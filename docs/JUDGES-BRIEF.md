# HIMGAURAV · The two-minute project brief

## The opening

“A mountain community should not need to understand a technical dashboard to report what it sees. And a reviewer should not have to decide without knowing where the information came from. HIMGAURAV connects these two needs.”

## The problem

Local observations, rainfall context and specialist research information serve different purposes. Presenting them without clear labels risks confusing an observation with a prediction, or a model output with a field measurement.

## The response

HIMGAURAV separates a simple community experience from a restricted operations and research experience. People can explore local context, prepare and submit observations. Authorized reviewers can assess reports, seek clarification and control what becomes public.

## The demonstration story

1. **Start with the community home.** Explain the selected place, source labels and the distinction between artwork and current conditions.
2. **Explore the map.** Explain approximate locations and why this is not safe-route navigation.
3. **Describe a report journey.** A draft is local; submission is deliberate; public publication is a separate decision.
4. **Describe human review.** Staff assess evidence and ask for clarification. Do not display real reports, accounts, access codes or private locations in a public demonstration.
5. **End with the evidence.** State the dated build/test results and the remaining validation needs.

## What is worth evaluating

- Whether community users can understand the interface and reporting flow.
- Whether source, freshness, uncertainty and LIVE/DEMO distinctions are clear.
- Whether sensitive submissions remain separate from public observations.
- Whether the review workflow helps authorized people make accountable decisions.

## What has been checked

The v9.11 publication recorded a successful web build, 19 science/data tests and 18 backend tests. Android emulator captures show the community home and map. These are engineering checks—not field validation, independent security certification or a guarantee of provider availability.

## Questions judges may ask

**Does it predict landslides?**

It is a research and decision-support prototype. No independently validated operational prediction claim is made.

**Does it issue government warnings?**

No. Community observations and experimental research outputs must not impersonate official warnings.

**Why both web and Android?**

A shared application foundation supports mobile community use and larger-screen review. Device-specific behavior still needs validation.

**Is it free forever and always online?**

No such guarantee is made. Free-tier services have limits, and availability depends on the hosting setup and external providers.

**Where is the source?**

The implementation is maintained privately. This repository is an intentionally limited public showcase.

## The next milestone

Field usability research, physical-device verification, end-to-end notification checks, independent security review and scientific validation should precede broader operational use.

## The close

“HIMGAURAV’s contribution is a clearer path from a local observation to reviewed evidence—with a community interface people can understand and boundaries that remain visible.”
